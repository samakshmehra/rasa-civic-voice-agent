# Civico Demo FAQ

## What problems can I report on this line?

Six categories: potholes and road damage, water supply, garbage collection,
street lights, drainage, and stray animals. Anything else needs the ward office.

This line does not handle emergencies. For a fire, an accident, a medical
emergency, or violence, call 112.

## How long will my complaint take?

This demo uses fictional target times by category, counted in working days from
the day a complaint is registered. They demonstrate deterministic routing and
are not promises from a real municipal authority.

## What is my complaint number?

Every registered complaint gets a reference like CIV1001. Keep it — it is the
fastest way to get a status update. You can also look up a complaint by the area
it was filed in.

## What do the statuses mean?

- **Registered** — received, not yet given to a field team
- **Assigned** — a field team from the department has it
- **In progress** — work has started
- **Resolved** — the department has closed it, with a note explaining what was done
- **Rejected** — the complaint was not accepted, with a reason

## Someone already reported my problem. What happens?

If an open complaint already exists for the same problem in the same neighbourhood, your
report is added to it rather than creating a duplicate. You get the existing
reference number. If you believe it is a genuinely different problem, say so and
a separate complaint is filed.

## My complaint says resolved but the problem is still there.

Tell us. The ward office can reopen it. Ask for a callback and an officer will
review the resolution note against what you are seeing.

## Why was my complaint rejected?

The most common reason is that the location falls under a different authority —
for example a lake, a highway, or railway land. The rejection note gives the
specific reason.

## Do I need to register or log in?

No. You do not need an account to report a civic problem. A contact number is
taken only so the department can call back.

## How do you know where I am?

You give a nearby landmark, road, or layout name and it is looked up on the map
to find your neighbourhood. Streets, layouts, malls and main roads work best.
Small shops and villages may not be on the map — give a bigger landmark nearby
if the first one is not found. The neighbourhood is always read back to you
before the complaint is filed.
